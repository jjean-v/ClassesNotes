## The ecological footprint

Is a substainable indicator that measures:

* The amount of biologically productive land and sea area required to provide the resources a population consumes and to absorb the waste it generates, especially CO₂ emissions.
* It is expressed in **global hectares (gha)**, a standardized
unit that represents one hectare of world-average
biological productivity

The Ecological Footprins compares:

* **Human demand**
* **Earth's Supply capacity**

The Ecological Footprint includes six main categories:

* Carbon footprint (largest component) – forest area needed to absorb CO₂ emissions
* Cropland – food and fiber production
* Grazing land – livestock production
* Fishing grounds – marine resource extraction
* Forest products – timber and wood use
* Built-up land – urban infrastructure

Global Situation - 2025

* Average global footprint per person ≈ 2.7–2.8 gha
* Available biocapacity per person ≈ 1.6 gha

## Planet Boundaries

* The Planetary Boundaries framework defines a
safe operating space for humanity within which
Earth-system processes remain stable, similar to
Holocene conditions.
* Crossing these boundaries increases the risk of
nonlinear, abrupt, or irreversible environmental
change

** 2009: 7 boundaries => 3 crossed
** 2023: 9 boundaries => 6 crossed


### 1. CO~2~ Driven Climate change

Main indicator:

* Atmospheric CO₂ concentration (ppm)
* Radiative forcing (W/m~2~)

Boundary value:

* CO₂ < 350 ppm
* Radiative forcing < +1 W/m~2~

Main causes:

* Fossil fuel combustion
* Deforestation
* Industrial processes

Main effects:

* Ice sheet instability
* Sea-level rise
* Extreme weather intensification
* Climate tipping elements

### 2. Ocean identifications

Main indicator:
*
* Aragonite saturation state (Ωarag)

Boundary value:

* ≥ 80% of pre-industrial level

Main causes:

* CO₂ absorption by oceans

Main effects:

* Coral reef degradation
* Shell-forming organism collapse
* Fisheries disruption

When CO~2~dissolves in seawater

* Carbon dioxide reacts with water to form **carbonic acid H~2~C0~3~**

Consequences:

* Lower Seawater **pH**
* Reduce the concentration of **carbonates ions**

### 3. Ozone Depletion

Main indicator:

* Ozone concentration (Dobson Units)

Boundary value:

* < 5% reduction from pre-industrial levels

Main causes:

* CFCs and halogenated compounds

Main effects:

* Increased UV radiation
* Human health risks
* sEcosystem damage

### 4. Excessive flows of nitrogen and phosphorous

#### Nitrogen Cycle

Main indicator:

* Industrial & agricultural fixation of N₂ (Tg N/year)

Boundary value:

* 35 Tg N/year

Main causes:

* Synthetic fertilizers

Intensive agriculture

* Fossil fuel combustion

Main effects:

* Eutrophication (Over-production of algue)
* Dead zones
* Greenhouse gas emissions (N₂O)
* Soil acidification

#### Phosphorous Cycle

Main indicator:

* Phosphorus inflow to oceans (relative to natural background)

Boundary value:

* < 10× natural background weathering

Main causes:

* Fertilizer runoff
* Wastewater discharge
* Soil erosion

Main effects:

* Ocean anoxia
* Marine ecosystem collapse
* •Harmful algal blooms

Solutions:

* Find more eco-friendly fertilizer
* Use a different way of using the fertilizer
** For now they are spraying, more is better
** Different way, may be more efficient and needs less fertilize for same performance
